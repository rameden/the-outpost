This came out of the readiness review for our SOC 2 Type I audit and ISO 27001 certification. The reviewer signed in with an old test account and voided an invoice. That account has no role at all.

## What happens

An account whose role is missing, or is a role we never defined, is allowed to do everything: read, create, send, mark paid, void and export. Accounts with a real role are held to it correctly: a viewer can only read, and a clerk cannot void.

## How to reproduce

1. Take an account with no role (the accounts made before roles existed have none).
2. Ask whether it may void an invoice.

The answer is yes. It is also yes for a made-up role such as "contractor", for every action.

## What should happen

Access is denied unless a role grants it. An account with no role, or with a role that isn't one of ours, can do nothing, not even read. Those accounts get a role from an admin before they are used again; that clean-up is tracked separately and is not part of this ticket.

## Controls this is evidence for

- SOC 2, CC6.3: access is given by role, on the principle of least privilege
- ISO/IEC 27001:2022, Annex A 5.15 (access control) and A 8.3 (information access restriction)

## Acceptance criteria

- An account with no role is refused every action
- An account with a role that isn't defined is refused every action
- Viewers, clerks and admins can do exactly what they can do today
- The tests for this ticket pass and are no longer marked as open
