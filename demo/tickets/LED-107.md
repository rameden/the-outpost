This came out of the readiness review for our SOC 2 Type I audit and ISO 27001 certification. A receipt for a customer in the UK showed their whole bank account number, and so did the audit trail entry for that payment.

## What happens

Account numbers are meant to show only their last four characters. That works for a number made of digits alone, such as 0012345678, which shows as "•••• 5678". An international number with letters in it (an IBAN), or any number typed with spaces or dashes, is shown exactly as it was entered.

## How to reproduce

1. Mark an invoice paid by bank transfer from account GB29 NWBK 6016 1331 9268 19.
2. Read the payment line on the receipt, and the note on the audit trail entry.

Both read "Bank transfer from account GB29 NWBK 6016 1331 9268 19".

## What should happen

Only the last four characters of an account number are ever shown, whatever it is made of: "•••• 6819" for the number above. Spaces and dashes are not part of the number and are ignored. A number of four characters or fewer is hidden completely ("••••"), since showing its last four would show all of it.

## Controls this is evidence for

- SOC 2, C1.1: confidential information is identified and kept confidential
- ISO/IEC 27001:2022, Annex A 8.11 (data masking)

## Acceptance criteria

- An account number with letters in it shows only its last four characters
- Spaces and dashes in an account number make no difference to what is shown
- A number of four characters or fewer is hidden completely
- The audit trail entry for a payment never holds a whole account number
- Numbers made of digits alone are shown as they are today
- The tests for this ticket pass and are no longer marked as open
