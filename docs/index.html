Mae, My Alter Ego

Your alter ego has a crew.

Mae is a small guild of agents that takes on your work the way you'd want it done. The Quartermaster turns your request into a plan, waits for your OK, then sends a Scout, a Scribe and an Artificer out on quests, several at once, with a Warden to review what changed. Each one keeps its own memory, its own workspace and its own limits.

The name

Mae is short for My Alter Ego.

It's the crew you'd be if there were more of you. They plan before they act, show you the plan, and nothing leaves the camp without your click. The orb is Mae's own light. It meets you at the door when you sign in to mission control, and it has two moods: at rest and at work.

See the orb

My

It runs on your machine, on your Claude subscription or your own API key. The camp is a folder of files you can open and read, and one note about you is shared by the whole crew.

Alter

Not one assistant but five other pairs of hands: a planner, a researcher, a writer, a coder and a reviewer, with up to four quests out at once.

Ego

Each one has a fixed identity. It grows its memory, but it can't rewrite who it is, and it can't talk its way past the camp rules.

The crew

Five specialists who know their trade, and stay in it.

Each crew member has a fixed identity. They grow their memory, but they can't rewrite who they are. Ask one for work outside its role and it declines in one line, naming the right teammate. Not a guild person? A theme pack (Settings → General) renames the five, for you and for them: Starship makes them a Commander, Navigator, Comms Officer, Engineer and Security Chief, on a ship flying missions. Newsroom is an Editor and staff filing stories, Studio a Director and a film crew shooting scenes, Lab a Lab Lead and a team running experiments. A Wizard you've added is renamed with them (a Test Pilot, on the ship). None of them fit? New pack… on the same page makes one of your own, with your names, icons and words for the five; it's kept in camp/packs.json, and it's there after a restart. Who they are and what they remember stays the same. Decorations, on the same page, are only for looks: snow in winter, bats at Halloween, confetti at New Year, with a string of lights under the top bar. They're off until you turn one on, and they change nothing the crew reads.

How an expedition runs

You approve the plan. The crew does the walking.

1

Ask

Type a request in mission control or the terminal camp. If it could mean two things, the Quartermaster asks you one short question first.

2

Approve the plan

The plan fills in step by step. Approve it and start, or approve it now and start it later from Home. Change it, or set a budget for the whole expedition.

3

Quests set out

Up to four run at once. Each adventurer starts clean from a written brief, so nobody drags the whole conversation along.

4

Reports come home

The Quartermaster reviews each report, sends a failed check back to be fixed, can have the Warden look over the change, and hands you one next step.

Orbit

The same sky, live, with your crew in it.

Orbit is mission control at full screen. The Quartermaster is the sun and each crew member a planet that lights up while it works. A dispatch is a comet going out, a report is a comet coming home, and each quest out is a moon. Open it from the top bar, the command palette or /orbit.

Watch

Click a planet and the camera flies to it and stays there. Its panel shows each quest, how long it has been out, its last few steps, and a reply as it's written. The sun's panel shows the running expeditions and where each plan stands.

Act

Answer a question, steer a quest, stop one or send new work from the panel, without leaving the view. Stopping a whole expedition asks you once first.

Replay

Scrub back through the day on a timeline. Demo plays a pretend camp, so you can see it busy without sending anyone anywhere. HD draws the system in three dimensions, as worlds you can make your own.

Trail map

The plan has a full-screen view of its own, beside Orbit in the top bar or with /trail. Each step is a waypoint with who was sent on it and how it stands, and a line runs from a step to each step that has to wait for it. It fills in as the crew works, and replays the day the same way. Whole map (or M) puts every plan on one map, drawn one of two ways, and Tree and Rings (or R) swap between them. As rings, the way Orbit draws the crew: each plan is a ring round one centre, the first asked innermost, so the map grows outward as the day goes on. Its steps are waypoints on the ring, with whoever was sent on one beside it, and the plans of one conversation start on the same bearing and are joined, ring to ring. As a tree: one trunk for the day, newest at the tip, and each plan a bough growing off it. Either way it's drawn as small as it takes to fit, and the way you chose is remembered. In a replay a plan that joins is drawn in (a ring opens out, a bough grows) while the rest move over for it, and one that goes when you wind back fades where it stood.

The kit

Everything in the pack.

Mission control

A local dashboard for the whole camp

npm run ui opens it on 127.0.0.1. Conversations filed under projects, a live feed, approvals as buttons, a file pane, a terminal along the bottom, and a layout you arrange yourself.

  • Steer or stop a run mid-quest, and resume it later
  • While a run works, Send becomes Stop, and Enter queues your next message behind it
  • Tool calls fold into one line per stretch ("Read 5 files, ran 2 commands"), and a Condensed/Detailed switch brings back every line
  • Attach a file, a folder, or a .zip that arrives as the folder it holds, up to 5,000 files
  • Mention @scribe anywhere in a plain chat and the Scribe takes that turn
  • Worktrees and linked folders for work you want kept apart
  • Changes you can review and undo
  • Sign in once per browser. A tab left open carries on when the camp restarts
Memory

Three layers that last

Each agent's own memory, a shared wiki with sources, and skills you approve. When a memory outgrows its room, a prompt gets the lines you pinned, the newest, and the ones that bear on the request. recall finds the rest, and nothing is tidied without your say. Type /constellation to see it all as a map: every line a star that grows each time it's used, where you can pin, forget or merge it.

Auto, the router

A router picks the model for each task

Set a crew member, a project or one conversation to Auto: Economy, Balanced or Quality. Right before each dispatch the router sizes up the task (how hard it is, how much is at stake, how big the request is), rules out every model that can't carry it, and picks the cheapest that should finish, or the likeliest to.

  • It keeps a model through routine work, and moves on when one is failing, rate limited or too small for the conversation
  • An outage is waited out, not given up on, and a model you pick yourself stays pinned
  • It learns from how each run actually went, within a limit, so one good or bad day doesn't decide a model's fate
  • Every run says which model it used and why. Settings → Models shows how Auto's runs have gone, with a switch for each model Auto may pick (you can still pick one it may not)
While you're away

It keeps an eye on the work

Each part has its own switch under Settings, and nothing it starts goes ahead without your OK.

  • Scheduled jobs report to an inbox and a macOS notification, and stay quiet when there's nothing new
  • A morning brief each weekday on the Today page: what's waiting on you, what's in motion, what's next
  • Your pull requests are followed, and new review comments or a failed check come back as a plan to approve
  • A weekly retro proposes what's worth remembering from the week's corrections, and drafts a how-to for work you keep asking for. Nothing is kept until you approve it
  • A night shift, off until you switch it on, uses the quiet hours to draft memory tidies, list old lines worth a look and note what housekeeping could clear. It only drafts and reports: the morning's Today page shows what it left, and nothing changes until you approve it
  • A scoreboard counts how much of what you typed was deciding, and how much was operating the pipeline, and a weekly chronicle shows whether the camp is paying off
Projects & Jira

From a note to a pull request

Jot a note and the Scout can write it up as a ticket with acceptance criteria, or ask for ideas and get up to five from the project's record. Start from a Jira ticket, step by step. Pull requests go out with a real description, from your own button, and review comments come back for you to read.

Tickets & debriefs

Tickets of its own, and a look back when one's done

No Jira? Mae keeps its own tickets, keyed MAE-1, MAE-2 and on, on a Tickets page. Turn a note or a plan into tickets with one button, or let the Quartermaster make them.

  • A ticket moves to In progress when work starts, and to Done when its pull request merges
  • When a ticket is done, a Mae ticket or a Jira one, the smallest model reads how the crew did on it: the plan, each quest, what reviews and checks caught, what you had to ask for twice
  • It proposes lessons, and nothing is kept until you approve it. Switch it on under Settings → What Mae does unasked
Voice

Read aloud

Connect an ElevenLabs key under Settings → Voice and Mae reads to you in the voice you choose: an answer, a job's report, an inbox item, a ticket's debrief, or text you select. One player in the top bar pauses, skips and changes speed, and stays as you move around. Each piece is kept, so hearing it again costs nothing.

Housekeeping

It tidies up when you say

Ask the Quartermaster to clean up the camp. It lists what could go (unused uploads, finished worktrees, leftovers from a crash), then asks before each step and shows exactly what will be removed.

Built for every day

Leave it running

The camp's own files are written whole or not at all, so a crash can't leave one half-written. A reply cut off by a dropped connection is asked for again. Stop the camp and its running work stops with it, with a Resume button waiting on each run.

  • A run picked up again, even on another model, never repeats a command, pull request or review
  • A settings file with a typo is set aside, not silently replaced
  • Checks you list run after every change, and a failure goes back to be fixed
  • Budgets hold even on a model too new to have a listed price
Skills

How-tos the crew picks up

After a good expedition the Quartermaster can propose a skill: the steps, the format, the pitfall that cost a retry. It goes live only when you approve it. Import a skill pack, or set one to apply to everything a crew member does.

Camp rules

Clear limits, kept by the camp's own code.

An agent can't talk its way past these. The camp's own source is read-only to the crew, so nothing it reads can loosen them.

Secrets stay put

File tools never read .env, private keys or credentials, at any depth, in a linked folder or through a symlink.

Shell asks first

Commands need your OK, stop after two minutes, and stop when you press Stop. A sandbox is there if you want it, on macOS or Linux. God mode, for the camp or one project, runs every command without asking, but "Never run" rules still stop one, and a scheduled job gets the sandbox instead.

Read-only git

Everyone can run status, diff, log and blame. Nobody pushes on their own.

No trampling

Parallel quests can't overwrite each other. A file changed by another quest has to be read again first.

Pages are information

Web pages, files and connector results are never treated as instructions, and suspected prompt injection is flagged.

Budgets hold

Spending is tallied after every reply, across all quests, and the run stops when the budget is reached.

Commands travel light

Your API keys and tokens are left out of every command and connector the crew starts, unless you name one to pass on.

The key isn't in the link

The link the camp prints signs one browser in, once. The camp's key never sits in an address, your history or a bookmark.

Deleting asks first

Uploads, worktrees and old records are cleared only when you ask, a step at a time, each showing what will go.

Get started

Pitch camp in three commands.

Needs Node 22.9 or later and either a Claude subscription or an API key. On a subscription, the crew runs through Claude Code with your account, costs show as estimates, and a chip in the header says when a run is drawing on extra-usage credit.
